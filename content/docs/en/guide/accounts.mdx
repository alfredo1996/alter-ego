Accounts represent the places where your money lives — bank accounts, credit cards, investment portfolios, loans, and cash on hand. Every transaction belongs to exactly one account, and your dashboard balances are computed from these accounts.

## Account Types

When you create an account you choose one of the following types:

| Type | Counts as | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Checking | Asset | Everyday spending account |
| Savings | Asset | High-yield savings, emergency fund |
| Cash | Asset | Physical cash, petty cash |
| Investment | Asset | Brokerage, retirement accounts |
| Other | Asset | Gift cards, prepaid accounts |
| Credit Card | Liability | Visa, Mastercard |
| Loan | Liability | Mortgage, auto loan, student loan |

Assets are the accounts with a positive balance, liabilities the ones with a negative balance (money you owe); net worth is the sum of all balances.

## Creating an Account

1. Navigate to the **Accounts** page from the sidebar.
2. Tap the **Add Account** button.
3. Fill in the account name, select a type, and enter the current balance.
4. Tap **Save**.

The account will appear immediately in your account list and on the dashboard.

## Editing an Account

Tap the account card to open the detail page, then use the **Edit** button to change the account name, type, or balance.

## Deleting an Account

Open the account you want to remove and tap **Delete**. You'll be asked to confirm. Deleting an account does not delete the transactions associated with it — they become orphaned. Consider reassigning transactions to another account before deleting.

## Account Detail Page

Tap any account card to see its detail view:

- **Balance Over Time** — an SVG chart showing how the account balance has changed.
- **Recent Transactions** — a filtered list of transactions belonging to this account.

You can use this page to quickly review activity on a single account without having to filter the main Transactions page manually.

## How Balances Work

Your account balance is the initial balance you entered when creating the account, adjusted by every transaction recorded against it:

- **Income** transactions increase the balance.
- **Expense** transactions decrease the balance.
- **Transfers** decrease the source account and increase the destination account.

If your balance looks wrong, check for missing, duplicate, or miscategorized transactions.

## Tips

- **Name accounts clearly** — "Chase Checking" is more useful than "Account 1" when you have several accounts.
- **Use the right type** — net worth follows the sign of each balance, so an overdrawn account counts as money you owe and an overpaid card as money owed to you.
- **Review regularly** — compare your Alter Ego balances to your actual bank balances periodically to catch any discrepancies.
